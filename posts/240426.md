i pretty much made a lot of the mistakes any junior would make, and thinking back i cringe at a lot of the moments that happened in the review. but hey, that means there is opportunity for growth. if i'm not looking back and cringing at myself, that means i'm staying stagnant.

- basics
  - make sure the technology works and the meeting room can join your meeting
  - no passcode or access issues. make it seamless for people, and tag people since they're busy and were in flow. send a reminder that the meeting is happening
  - know what you're talking about, and don't half-ass any part of the work
- how to answer questions
  - if you don't know how to answer a question, admit it, don't try to worm your way around it
  - any silence between their question and your answer, even a second, shows uncertainty, signals you didn't look into things enough, and is not a great sign of a good engineer. if you don't know, say it. if you think someone else can answer better, say it. don't just choke and say nothing
  - after a question is answered, find a good way to transition. you're leading the meeting, you direct the show, so show people where to go
- how to end a design review
  - list out the next steps
  - say your learnings and summarize
  - i saw we went 5 minutes over and wanted to end quickly, but i didn't summarize things or write down the next steps
- postmortem of my design review
  - didn't question the status quo of what had already been built
  - didn't read enough into otel to answer the biggest question
  - didn't know that was the biggest question, so i went in with the wrong expectations and goal
  - never ever ever present something that is not in your own words. it looks very bad when people ask you to clarify it
  - speak out loud

visibility means exposure, and exposure means people can judge you, criticize you, and reject you at a scale that feels much more dangerous than quiet mediocrity, which is why so many talented people stay in the shadows building things nobody ever sees, telling themselves they just need a little more time to get it perfect.

feedback i got from E afterwards:

> Thanks for giving the design review Ben. Don't feel discouraged if it was bumpy—it was your first time doing it and your first real presentations, especially at a place like here, will always be a trial by fire. My main feedback (which I was given too as a PhD student) is you need to remember that no one besides me has any context on the thing you're presenting. You should assume they're starting from scratch, even J who's been involved in the brainstorming.
>
> For any presentation, it's important to start high level and progressively, slowly, drill down. That's why you always start with goals and the problem we're trying to solve or question we're trying to answer. Then you say what the key idea for the solution is. Then you get more specific etc
>
> In any case, it was a great effort and I appreciate your hard work!
