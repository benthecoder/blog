i almost didn't go to the good friday service because of work, and i was 35 minutes late, but i'm glad i still went.

i met D from puerto rico. a few things i learned about puerto rico:
- the first holy week in puerto rico was probably around 1510 at [caparra](https://www.elnuevodia.com/noticias/locales/notas/sabias-que-la-primera-celebracion-de-semana-santa-en-puerto-rico-habria-sido-en-caparra-a-principios-del-siglo-xvi/), the first spanish settlement on the island, but nobody knows for sure and some historians say it was in aguadilla instead
- good friday is an official holiday there, and one writer remembers it being treated as [more sacred than christmas](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/latino/voices-jesus-crying-memories-viernes-santos-island-n746251), with only religious tv, no loud music on the radio, and bacalao (salted cod) for dinner
- in the colonial era the churches used rattles and clappers instead of bells on thursday and friday
- towns like santurce and carolina still do a good friday way of the cross reenactment with all 14 stations

after sharing about malaysia and my lutheran background, i looked into where christianity in malaysia came from:
- malaysia wasn't colonized by just one power, so different christian branches arrived at different times
  - portugal → catholic
  - netherlands → protestant (reformed)
  - united kingdom → anglican, methodist, etc.
- today about [9.1% of malaysians are christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Malaysia) (2020 census), but it's 50.1% in sarawak and 24.7% in sabah
- the portuguese took malacca in 1511, the dutch in 1641, and the british took over in 1795
- parts of the bible were published in malay in the netherlands in 1629, one of the first east asian languages to get a bible translation
- [lutheranism in malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutheran_Church_in_Malaysia) mostly took off in 1953, when lutheran missionaries who'd been expelled from china started working with chinese families resettled into "new villages" during the malayan emergency
- the tamil and chinese lutherans ended up as separate churches in 1962 and 1963, and the lutheran church in malaysia now has about 6,700 baptised members
