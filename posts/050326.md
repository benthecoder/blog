it's quite scary to think how AI can replace all of us one day. day by day AI is getting more capable and more efficient. if ai could do what humans can do but 100x cheaper and faster and better, what is the point of humans? what occupation, if any, will ever be safe from AI? trade jobs? what if robotics has its ChatGPT moment as well?

there are countless ways to try to approach this problem, so how does anthropic go about it?

their framework starts by breaking jobs into tasks. instead of asking "will AI take this job?", they look at tasks such as writing code, reviewing code, and attending meetings. then they ask two questions:

1. can AI do it? known as theoretical capability, would an LLM roughly halve the time it takes?
2. is AI actually doing it? this is the observed exposure. they checked real Claude usage to see whether people are using AI for that task.

then they added up the task scores for each job, weighted by how much time the job spends on each task.

their conclusion:

1. the gap is big. AI could theoretically do 94% of computer and math tasks, but is only doing 33% of it.
2. most exposed? programmers (75% of tasks), customer service reps, data entry, financial analysts
3. least exposed? 30% of workers have zero measured exposure, such as cooks, bartenders, lifeguards, motorcycle mechanics, and dishwashers.
4. exposed workers are more educated and better paid. they earn about 47% more on average.
5. unemployment has not risen for the most exposed workers, but there are early signs that hiring of 22- to 25-year-olds into exposed jobs has slowed.

my thoughts:

1. writing code by hand is obviously dead, i've questioned my value as a programmer as AI gets better, asking myself how i can serve the AI better rather than how the AI can serve me better for my work
2. i don't see myself being a programmer for too long, much of the joy and satisfaction that came from spending hours and even days on a problem is gone, and now everyone is just getting burnt out by doing too much and still feeling like they're not doing enough.
3. i want to spend more time creating things in the real world, and less time doing everything digitally.
