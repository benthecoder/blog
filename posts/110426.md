til:
- seattle has the [world's first light rail on a floating bridge](https://www.seattletimes.com/seattle-news/transportation/light-rail-track-floating-bridge-i-90-sound-transit/)
  - the train crosses 1.2 miles over lake washington at 55 mph on the I-90 bridge, and it's remarkably quiet and smooth
  - floating bridges move: they're anchored to the lake bottom instead of resting on columns, and those shifts would wreck fixed rail. engineers invented "track bridges" using earthquake-retrofitting technology to keep the rails steady while the bridge flexes, and since they couldn't drill into the pontoons and keep them watertight, they used high-strength adhesive instead
  - the system is andy foan's curved element supported rail (CESuRa)
  - the plinth disaster: contractor kiewit-hoffman installed thousands of defective concrete plinths (the blocks that hold the rails) with wrong heights, voids, and faulty fasteners. the problems surfaced from 2019 to 2022, and sound transit ended up demolishing and rebuilding about 5,400–6,000 of them
