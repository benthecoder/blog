til that 60-80% of US carrot production is controlled by two companies, Grimmway Farms and Bolthouse Farms, and both are owned by private equity firms. ([Forbes](https://farmlandgrab.org/post/31861-inside-the-two-companies-that-dominate-the-us-carrot-crop), [PESP](https://pestakeholder.org/reports/behind-the-harvest-private-equity-and-worker-safety-at-grimmway-farms/))

how it got this way: baby carrots were invented in 1986 by a farmer in bakersfield named mike yurosek, and carrot eating took off. bolthouse had about half the US market by 1992. campbell soup sold it to butterfly equity in 2019 for $510 million, and grimmway was bought by teays river investments around 2020-21. one of bolthouse's co-ceos has called the setup the "OPEC of carrots" (the oil cartel, but for carrots).

a few details from those pieces:
- price. producer prices for carrots are up more than 40% since 2019 vs about 22% general inflation (BLS, via [forbes](https://web.archive.org/web/20260223174107/https://www.forbes.com/sites/chloesorvino/2023/11/21/inside-the-two-companies-that-dominate-the-us-carrot-crop/)).
- workers. PESP says grimmway has had 19 OSHA violations since 2021, 7 of them serious, and a federal judge found a pattern of disability discrimination against injured workers in 2025. they also report a farmworker, rosa sanchez, was killed in 2023 when a truck backed over her.
- water. in california's cuyama valley these two used 67% of the local water in 2022, around 9.6 billion gallons (forbes).
- who owns the owners. teays river is backed by large US pension funds, so some of this is probably somebody's retirement money.
