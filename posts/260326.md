fun facts about fairchild:
- it opened on march 23, 1938 in coral gables, on 83 acres bought by [colonel robert montgomery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Hiester_Montgomery), a retired accountant. he named it after his friend instead of himself
- the friend was [dr. david fairchild](https://fairchildgarden.org/mission-history/), maybe the greatest plant explorer in american history. in 1897 he started the usda's office of seed and plant introduction, and he went to every continent except antarctica looking for useful plants
- he's credited with bringing [about 30,000 plant species and varieties](https://www.usda.gov/about-usda/news/blog/discovery-could-rekindle-interest-usda-trailblazer) to the us, including mangos, soybeans, alfalfa, nectarines, dates, avocados, and the [cherry blossom trees dc is famous for](https://naturalhistory.si.edu/research/botany/news/plant-press/how-two-key-figures-brought-cherry-trees-us) now
- not all of it went well. he saw farmers in japan feeding kudzu to animals, so in 1902 he grew kudzu seedlings at his home near dc and "scattered them about rather recklessly." it started smothering everything, and by 1938 he was [warning people about it](https://www.popsci.com/environment/plant-hunters-usda/), but the government was already paying southern farmers to plant it. it's now known as the vine that ate the south
- on opening day he planted [a baobab](https://fairchildgarden.org/visit/the-gardens-early-plantings/) from seeds he collected in west africa in 1927. it's the only one of the eleven trees planted that day that's still alive, near the sunken garden. the even bigger baobab by the gate house was planted the same year from a county nursery
- in 1940, at 71, he led the garden's first collecting trip, sailing from the philippines to indonesia on a chinese junk called the [cheng ho](https://fairchildgarden.org/mission-history/description-of-collections/the-cheng-ho-expedition-blog/members-of-the-cheng-ho-expedition/). his wife marian, alexander graham bell's daughter, came along and collected shells
- the garden was designed by [william lyman phillips](https://fairchildgarden.org/mission-history/landscape-design/), who worked with the olmsted brothers, the firm behind central park. he designed the palm section by grouping palms by genus
- the palm and cycad collections are some of the best in the world. [cycads go back about 280 million years](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/many-of-these-plants-older-than-dinosaurs-face-extinction-180979704/), older than the dinosaurs, though [the species alive today are only around 12 million years old](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/living-fossil-cycad-plants-are-actually-evolutions-comeback-kings)
- it has more than 3,400 species now. on its first anniversary it had [692 plants of 243 species](https://fairchildgarden.org/visit/the-gardens-early-plantings/)
