today we voyage to marco island, once home to the calusa, a powerful native american maritime society of expert fishermen and gatherers. i don't know much about the island, but our goals were simple and pure: go shelling, see alligators, swim at the beach, and relax.

we picked up the car from the airport and stopped at pho 79. i learned about culantro, which is cilantro's bolder cousin and is preferred for pho because it holds up better in hot broth without going limp.

driving through the everglades is like following a laser beam: 2 hours of one monotonous line with a canal on the right. we stopped at publix and bought t-shirts, a frisbee, postcards, juice and snacks, as asian tourists should.

we checked into our airbnb, a cozy blue home on a tidal residential canal. i love airbnbs with art and decor that fit the theme of the place. it puts us in the mood and mindset of travel and vacation.

![](/images/020526-img-9870.jpg)

![](/images/020526-img-9872.jpg)

we drove down to tigertail beach, realized it's basically a lagoon, and went to marco's south beach public access instead. the beach was quiet and peaceful, the weather was perfect, the water was warm. i got in the water with my dad, and i even mustered up the courage to swim in open water. it was not pleasant, of course, but it was fun to try.

![shells](/images/020526-shells.jpg)

![welcome sign](/images/020526-marco-island.jpg)

![south beach](/images/020526-beach.jpg)

dinner was at deep lagoon seafood. it started to rain and we had no choice but to valet, which always feels weird to us as asians, and especially hard for my dad, who would never pay for expensive parking in malaysia. we had to wait and the service was bad. i ordered crab legs but they brought snow crab, so i switched to stone crab, and the meat was powdery. it was an absolute disappointment. the only good dish was the crab cake.

before heading back for the night, we explored winn-dixie for snacks and groceries.
